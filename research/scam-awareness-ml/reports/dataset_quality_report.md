## 1. Executive Summary

This report provides a formal data audit of the canonical dataset (`data/scam_messages_dataset.csv`) constructed for binary scam classification and pedagogical scam category recognition within the WiseCub platform.

The dataset strictly satisfies all academic constraints:
- **Total Rows:** `2,500` unique records (exactly on target in the required 2,000–3,000 range).
- **Class Balance:** Perfectly balanced `50.0% / 50.0%` (1,250 scam/suspicious vs. 1,250 legitimate/benign).
- **Zero-Tolerance Duplicates:** `0` exact duplicates and `0` cross-group near-duplicate leaks.
- **Complete Integrity:** `0` missing values, `0` null fields across all 12 columns.
- **Group Isolation:** Organized into `253` distinct template groups to prevent data leakage during model training.

## 3. Data Provenance & Legal Licensing Separation

To ensure scientific honesty and avoid manufacturing empirical claims, the corpus clearly separates real-world public data from educational synthetic data:

```
Total Dataset: 2,500 Rows
├── Public Real-World Benchmark: 600 Rows (24.0%)
│   ├── Public SMS Spam: 300 rows (UCI SMS Spam Collection, Almeida et al., 2012)
│   └── Public Benign Ham: 300 rows (UCI NUS & Tagg subsets)
└── WiseCub Educational Synthetic Corpus: 1,900 Rows (76.0%)
    ├── Synthetic Scam Categories: 950 rows (Phishing, QR, Impersonation, Financial)
    └── Synthetic Benign Youth Messages: 950 rows (School LMS, Banking, 2FA, Family)
```

### 3.1 Public Benchmark Records (UCI SMS Spam Collection)
- **Source:** University of California, Irvine (UCI) Machine Learning Repository.
- **Reference:** Almeida, T.A., Gómez Hidalgo, J.M., & Yamakami, A. (2012). *SMS Spam Collection*. DOI: 10.24432/C5CC84.
- **License:** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (`CC-BY-4.0`).
- **Composition:** 300 spam texts (UK forums) and 300 legitimate messages (student volunteers from the National University of Singapore and UK SMS corpora).
- **Limitation:** Historical collection (2005–2010); lacks modern youth-targeted threats such as quishing (QR codes) or gaming crypto airdrops.

### 3.2 Synthetic Educational Records (WiseCub Corpus v1.0)
- **Authoring:** Deterministically generated by WiseCub Scam Safety research modules.
- **Reference:** WiseCub Scam Awareness Curriculum Levels 1–6.
- **License:** Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International (`CC-BY-NC-4.0`).
- **Composition:** 1,900 examples spanning modern student scenarios (school cloud quotas, Discord/Roblox social engineering, crypto task schemes, real pocket money alerts).
- **Design Rule:** Specifically introduces "hard negative" legitimate messages mentioning money, links, and security codes to prevent the model from learning shallow keywords.

## 5. Potential Sources of Bias & Ethical Limitations

1. **Language Monolingualism:** The dataset is currently 100% English. It does not represent Sinhala or Tamil scam vectors or code-mixed "Singlish" expressions.
2. **Synthetic Stylistic Consistency:** Synthetic messages generated from parameterized templates exhibit regular syntactic patterns. While template-group partitioning prevents split leakage, neural models or n-gram classifiers can memorize subtle token co-occurrences.
3. **No Private Student Data:** In accordance with institutional research ethics, zero private student data, real passwords, OTPs, or live bank credentials were used.
